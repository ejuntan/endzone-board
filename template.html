▸ Model, data & backtest

These are projections for the upcoming slate — 2026 Week 2, 15 games kicking off 2026-09-21. Each player's numbers come from their usage through the season so far, blended with last year, using only information available before kickoff.

1 · Scoring rates, fit from data. Instead of guessed coefficients, each opportunity is weighted by an empirical touchdown rate estimated from 2021–22 play-by-play, split by exact field position:

Rushing, by yard lineTD rateReceiving targetTD rate
Goal line (≤2)End-zone, ≤5
Inside-5 (3–5)End-zone, 6–10
Inside-10 (6–10)Red-zone, short of EZ

Midfield (21–50)Deep, short of EZ

A goal-line carry scores about half the time; a carry from the 11–20 barely 1-in-20. That granularity is the core fix — a single flat red-zone rate blurs a 25× difference.

2 · Opportunity & context features. The analytic estimate is one input among many. The model also sees each player's red-zone and goal-line carry share, red-zone target share, end-zone usage, snap share, carry/target volume, recent scoring rate, position, and the game's Vegas implied team total — all trailing, pre-kickoff numbers.

3 · Learned, calibrated probability. Those features feed a gradient-boosted classifier, isotonic-calibrated so the output probabilities match observed scoring rates. It is trained only on seasons before each test year, and players ruled Out or Doubtful are removed with their red-zone workload reallocated to available teammates. Tiers group the result: Elite ≥ 45%, Strong ≥ 33%, Live ≥ 22%, Longshot below.

4 · Out-of-sample backtest (multi-season, walk-forward). Each test season is scored by a model fit only on seasons before it (2022←’21, 2023←’21-22, 2024←’21-23, 2025←’21-24), predictions made week-by-week on prior data only. Held-out totals: active skill-player games across four seasons, base rate .

Adding red-zone carry share and Vegas totals beats the plain opportunity model, which in turn crushes the naive “count past touchdowns” baseline — across four held-out seasons (21440 games), log loss 0.475 vs 0.599, AUC 0.69 vs 0.66. It ranks a scorer above a non-scorer 69% of the time, and its probabilities land within about 1.3 points of the real rate (mean calibration error).
